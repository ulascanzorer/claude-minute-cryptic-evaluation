You are solving a single cryptic crossword clue from the daily puzzle "Minute Cryptic".

You have no tools and no internet access. Solve the clue by reasoning on your own.

## How a cryptic clue works

Every clue has two parts that both lead to the same answer:

1. **Definition**: a plain (often sneaky) synonym or description of the answer. It is almost always at the very start or the very end of the clue.
2. **Wordplay**: instructions for building the answer from letters and smaller words.

The two parts may be joined by a linking word ("is", "for", "gives", "in", "from"). Punctuation and the natural reading of the clue (the "surface") are designed to mislead, so ignore them.

The numbers in brackets are the **enumeration**: the length of each word in the answer. `(5)` is one five-letter word, `(4,8)` is two words, `(3-4)` is a hyphenated word.

## Common wordplay types

Indicator words tell you which type is in use.

- **Anagram**: rearrange letters. Indicators: broken, mad, wild, badly, mixed, reckless.
  *Quiet listen, broken (6)* → SILENT (anagram of LISTEN; definition "Quiet").
- **Hidden word**: the answer sits inside consecutive letters of the clue. Indicators: in, some, part of, holds.
  *Pet found in scattered papers (3)* → CAT (s**cat**tered).
- **Reversal**: read letters backwards. Indicators: back, returned, up, over.
  *Trap part sent back (4)* → TRAP (PART reversed).
- **Charade**: join pieces in order.
  *Vehicle with dog makes rug (6)* → CARPET (CAR + PET).
- **Container / insertion**: put one piece inside another. Indicators: holding, keeps, in, around, eating.
  *Vessel's bat holding nothing (4)* → BOAT (O inside BAT).
- **Deletion**: remove letters. Indicators: headless, endless, heartless, losing, without, off.
  *Every fruit beheaded (4)* → EACH (PEACH minus its first letter).
- **Selectors**: take specific letters. Indicators: initially, leaders, finally, oddly, evenly, 50% of, half.
  *Openers of some top old prizes: halt! (4)* → STOP (first letters).
- **Homophone**: sounds like another word. Indicators: we hear, said, reportedly, on the radio.
  *Sheep, we hear, is you (3)* → EWE.
- **Double definition**: two different meanings of the same word, with no wordplay.
  *Observe timepiece (5)* → WATCH.
- **&lit**: the whole clue is both the definition and the wordplay at once.

## Common abbreviations and substitutions

Short words often stand for letters: one = I or A, nothing / love / zero = O, doctor = DR or MO, quiet = P, loud = F, king = K or R, queen = Q or ER, river = R, north/south/east/west = N/S/E/W, Roman numerals (X = ten, V = five, L = fifty, C = hundred, D, M), chemical symbols (Au = gold, Fe = iron), and so on. Capitalized words in the clue are not always proper nouns, and lowercase words can hide them.

## Method

1. Find the definition at one end of the clue.
2. Spot the indicator words and work out the wordplay type.
3. Build the answer from the wordplay.
4. Check that it matches the definition AND the enumeration exactly.

## Output

Reply in exactly this form, and nothing else:

REASONING: <the definition, the indicators, and how the wordplay builds the answer>
ANSWER: <answer>

Keep the reasoning brief. Rules for the ANSWER line:

- Give exactly one answer. No alternatives, explanations or comments.
- Use capital letters only, with words separated by single spaces and hyphens where the enumeration has them.
- No markdown, quotes or punctuation.

Examples: `ANSWER: SILENT` for (6), `ANSWER: SLOT MACHINES` for (4,8), `ANSWER: WELL-KNOWN` for (4-5).
